Terms

Robotics board
Strawbees Robotic Inventions board that clips to the micro:bit and drives the servo. Needs three AAA cells and a power switch.
Servo
Motor that turns to a coded position (0–100% in Strawbees blocks), not a free-spinning wheel.
Zero
Known start: flash “set servo A to 0%”, then nudge the horn. If one side will not budge, you are at 0%.
Linkage
Beams and joints that turn a servo push into boom motion. On this crane: green-beam joint to the yellow straw on the servo.
Friction joint
Joint held by tightness rather than a freeze lock. Secure the back or it walks under load.
Position range
The two numbers that move this crane without binding. They belong to this linkage, this slide, this boom — not to another table.
MakeCode
Microsoft block editor at makecode.microbit.org. Add the Strawbees Robotic Inventions extension before servo blocks appear.

The lift that coded fine

Fig. 1.0 · Consequence

A servo that hits 80% is useless if the yellow straw slips first.

What happened

A pair flashes button A → 80%. The horn twitches. The boom barely nods. They keep editing percentages. The recycling-sort demo still drops the load.

What failed

Not the number. The linkage from the green beam to the yellow straw was a friction fit that walked off. Code cannot lock a joint. First crane movement is a test, not a pass.

Three ways to change the lift

Fig. 2.0 · Core concept

  1. CodeMakeCode: 0%, a written range, then buttons or a position variableBoard
  2. MotorServo on the Robotics board — zero it, then slide it along the strawYou
  3. MechanismPrism, levers, friction joint, hook, linkage from green beam to yellow strawYou

Orange is hands. Purple is the program. Change one layer, then test. Changing all three at once hides the fault.

Mechanism — levers, not magic

Fig. 2.1 · Mechanism

The crane is a mechanical system. A servo angle only becomes a lift if joints and a linkage carry the push to the boom.

Stand

Prism on a trapezoid

Trapezoid base, then beams that make a prism. Click 1-leg connectors one at a time. A rocking tower eats every later test.

Boom

Joints that must move

Moving joints for the top, then the boom, then a hook from a joint. A friction joint holds pose — secure the back or it walks.

Drive

The linkage sentence

Connect the joint on the green beam to the yellow straw on the servo. That path is the machine. Miss it and the horn just waves.

Motor — known zero, then a beam

Fig. 2.2 · Motor

  1. 01Clip micro:bit, Robotics board, and servo. Align the black wire with the white arrow. Three AAA cells. Power on.
  2. 02USB to the computer. New project at makecode.microbit.org. Add the Strawbees Robotic Inventions extension. Pair the board.
  3. 03Flash a forever loop: set servo A position to 0%. After upload, nudge the horn. If one side will not budge, you are at zero.
  4. 04Only then clip a yellow straw beam to the horn. Sliding the servo along that straw later changes how hard the linkage pushes.
Technician rule

Do not hunt for a range until you have a known 0%. A “stuck” horn after this program is often the pass, not a fault.

Code — find this crane’s range

Fig. 2.3 · Code

The job

Experiment with servo positions until the boom travels without binding. Download several times. Write the two numbers on the bench. They belong to this linkage, this slide, this boom length.

Then, and only then

Buttons A and B jump to those two numbers. Later, a position variable creeps: change by +1 and −1, clamped to the range. A bigger change makes a faster crane.

Simulator lie

The on-screen micro:bit has no boom. Borrowed percentages from another table will bind this one. A green simulator is not a lift.

Worked example — Recycling Bay Crane

Fig. 3.0 · Named brief

Pairs. Strawbees STEAM Classroom Robotics — micro:bit. The load is a light classroom object, not a skip.

Must

  • Free-standing prism; boom with a hook.
  • Robotics board powered; black wire on the white arrow.
  • Servo zeroed at 0%; two range numbers written down.
  • A and B stay inside that range; hook lifts a light load once.

Build order

  • Prism → clip board and servo → USB, extension, pair, power.
  • Zero → yellow beam on horn → joints, boom, friction joint, hook.
  • Linkage to yellow straw → find range → buttons → optional ±1 variable.
  • Slide the servo; lock a slipping straw with a 1-leg connector.

Trade-offs and limits

Fig. 4.0 · Limits

Jump vs creep

Buttons to two endpoints are easy to demo. A position variable (±1) is slower and safer for a load. Raise the change amount only after the range is proven.

Code vs slide

New percentages and sliding the servo both change throw. Sliding is mechanical advantage. Do not retune both at once or you cannot say which fix worked.

Friction vs screws

A friction joint is fast to pose. Under a hook load it walks. Tiny screws hold a falling servo horn. Speed of build is not the same as a pass.

Straw lengths

Swap lengths on the boom or the moving lever and the same code will over-travel or under-lift. Range numbers die when the geometry changes.

Common faults

Fig. 4.1 · Helpdesk script

SymptomLikely causeFix
Yellow straw slips on the servo Friction only; no lock on the member Click a 1-leg connector over the straw
Horn falls off the linkage Arm seated on friction, not fastened Use the tiny screws; re-zero if you moved the horn
Horn will not nudge after 0% Servo is at end-stop (often a pass) or the .hex never landed Confirm upload; if one side is locked, zero is set
Boom binds or slams Range copied from another crane, or geometry changed Find this build’s two numbers again
Twitch, no lift Linkage missed, or servo not slid to a useful push Join green-beam joint to yellow straw; slide the motor
Board dead, no twitch Power, batteries, or cable polarity Three AAA; switch on; black wire to the white arrow
